The AUX
Building a community-centered website for healing, wellness, culture, and racial equity

The AUX is a commercial hub in Evanston dedicated to healing, wellness, and racial equity. The space brings together businesses led by members of historically marginalized groups across wellness, beauty, food, creative industries, and community care. The AUX describes itself as a community-owned space built around connection, collaboration, and community-powered development.

Tapestri supported The AUX with a WordPress website redesign that helped communicate the organization’s mission, showcase its tenants, support fundraising, and make the site easier to navigate and maintain.

The Challenge

The AUX needed a website that could do more than describe a physical space. It needed to tell the story of a community-centered project, introduce tenants, support an equity campaign, share news and press, and help visitors understand how to get involved.

The project also required careful collaboration with the client. The team needed to understand The AUX’s goals, existing materials, audience needs, communication style, and visual identity before making design and content decisions. The process included client interviews, analysis of the existing website, review of comparable sites, and study of The AUX’s public materials, including articles, videos, social media, and other marketing content.

Key needs included:

clearer storytelling about The AUX’s mission and purpose
a stronger structure for tenant profiles
support for fundraising and equity-investment information
visible calls to invest or donate throughout the site
a timeline and fundraising “thermometer”
a more organized news and press section
a design that followed the organization’s existing style guide
a maintainable WordPress site that could be launched securely
Our Approach

We followed a full software development lifecycle with a real client: discovery, design, development, testing, release, and maintenance planning. The project was also iterative, meaning the team created versions of the site, gathered client feedback, revised the design, and continued refining the product.

During discovery, the team analyzed The AUX’s current website and comparable community-centered spaces, including The Phoenix in Milwaukee. The team also reviewed The AUX’s existing marketing materials to understand what was already working and where the website could better support the organization’s goals.

The team then moved into design, beginning with low-fidelity prototypes in Google Slides to quickly explore page structure, messaging, and layout ideas. Later, the team used Figma, WordPress, Elementor, Royal Elementor, and ElementsKit to prototype and develop the site with a more polished visual direction.

What We Built
A clearer site structure

We helped organize the site around the most important information visitors would need: who The AUX is, what it does, its story, tenant profiles, news, contact information, event and meeting booking, parking information, and equity-investor FAQs. The live site now includes sections for “Who we are,” “What We Do,” “Our Story,” “Meet Our Tenants,” “News,” and “Contact.”

Tenant profiles

The AUX brings together a range of businesses and community partners. The site needed a way to feature those tenants clearly and give visitors a sense of the people, services, and experiences available in the space.

The team created tenant profile pages and organized tenant information so visitors could learn about businesses such as The Aux Wellness Collective, The Laundry Cafe, Chicky’s Kitchen Creations, Embrace Your Crown, Better Me 2 Wellness, Sokana Collective, Studio 8 Podcast, Sunshine Enterprises, and Wintrust.

Fundraising and equity-campaign support

Because fundraising and community ownership were central to the project, the website needed to make investment and donation opportunities visible. The team prioritized calls to invest or donate, added equity-campaign information, and incorporated fundraising-oriented features such as a timeline and thermometer-style progress indicator.

A more organized news section

After client feedback, the team restructured the news area to better showcase different types of updates, including press, featured stories, and general news. This helped The AUX communicate momentum, public recognition, and community activity more clearly.

A WordPress site ready for launch

The team built the site on a live WordPress development instance, then supported launch by helping the client move to a live hosting provider. This included exporting and importing the site to GoDaddy hosting and following a launch checklist focused on security, backups, and site readiness.

The Result

The final website gave The AUX a more organized and expressive digital home. The site better communicates The AUX’s identity as a hub for healing, wellness, culture, connection, and racial equity. It also gives visitors clearer paths to learn about tenants, explore events and meetings, read news, contact the organization, and understand opportunities to support the project.

The process also helped the team practice real-world client collaboration: asking questions, interpreting feedback, revising designs, managing follow-up, and balancing creative ideas with client priorities.
